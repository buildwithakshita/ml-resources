02
How it works

Five ideas, that's all

What it is, the dev version, and the trap

No training data and no right answers. Just "how far did you get?"

  • Sensorswhat the car sees

    Each car shoots out 5 rays (far right, right, ahead, left, far left) and measures how far it is to the edge of the road, up to 30 metres.

    Dev: 5 numbers between 0 and 1, the function's inputs

    Watch out: The car never sees the whole map. Only these 5 numbers.

    Input
    input = 1 - distance / 30 # 1 = wall right there # 0 = nothing in range

  • The braina tiny neural network

    5 inputs, 6 hidden neurons, 2 outputs: how much to steer and how fast to go. About 50 weights in total, written in plain JavaScript.

    Dev: A function with ~50 tunable numbers

    Watch out: At the start, every weight is random, so every car drives randomly.

    Shape
    5 sensors → 6 hidden → steer, speed
    hidden = relu(W1 · sensors + b1) steer = tanh(W2 · hidden + b2) speed = sigmoid(W2 · hidden + b2)

  • Fitnessthe only score

    Nobody tells a car how to drive. Each one just gets a score: how far along the road it got, plus a bonus for finishing a lap fast.

    Dev: One metric you're trying to push up

    Watch out: Cars find loopholes, like driving in circles, so going nowhere for 3 seconds counts as a crash.

    Score
    score = distance driven + bonus if it finished fast

  • Neuroevolutionsurvival of the best driver

    After each round, the best brains survive. The next 50 cars are copies of them with small random tweaks (mutations), plus a few totally new brains. Repeat.

    Dev: Random search that keeps what works

    Watch out: This is not gradient descent. No backprop, no loss function, just keep the winners.

    One generation
    drive all 50 cars keep the best brains next = mutate(best) × 50 repeat

  • Overfittingwhy it gets a new road every round

    Train on one road and the brain memorises that road instead of learning to drive. So every generation gets a brand new random road.

    Dev: Tests that only pass on your machine

    Watch out: A brain that looks perfect on its training road can be useless on a new one.

    Tested on 40 roads it had never seen
    one road: 11 / 40 laps
    new road every round: 40 / 40
Real robotaxis Companies like Waymo use far more sensors (cameras, lidar, radar), huge amounts of real driving data and many models working together. This is the tiny version of one idea: a brain that turns what it senses into steering.

Self-check

Say these out loud

If you can answer all five, you've got it

  1. What are the inputs and outputs of the car's brain?
  2. If nobody gives it the right answer, how does it improve?
  3. What's the difference between neuroevolution and gradient descent?
  4. Why train on a new road every generation?
  5. What would you add to make it drive in traffic?
