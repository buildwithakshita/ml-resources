01
The idea

Every show is a dot

Things you like live close together

Give every show a few scores (how funny, how feel-good) and each one becomes a dot on a map. To recommend something, look at the dots closest to a show you loved. That's K-nearest neighbours: the K is just how many neighbours you look at.

02
The code · runs in your browser

10 lines, your own recommender

scikit-learn's NearestNeighbors does the work

Open it in Colab, press Shift + Enter on each cell, then add your favourite show with your own scores and see what it recommends.

  • ~5 minutes
  • No install
  • Free
Open in Colab GoogleDownload notebook .ipynb
# the map: a few scores per show
features = ["funny", "feel_good", "romance", "crime"]

def recommend(title, cols=features, k=3):
    model = NearestNeighbors(n_neighbors=k + 1).fit(shows[cols])  # learn where every dot is
    i = shows.index[shows["show"] == title][0]                    # find your show
    _, idx = model.kneighbors(shows[cols].iloc[[i]])              # closest dots
    return [s for s in shows["show"].iloc[idx[0]] if s != title][:k]

recommend("Friends")
# → ['New Girl', 'The Office', 'How I Met Your Mother']
03
The catch

The map is only as smart as what you measure

KNN doesn't pick the features. You do.

If the only thing you track is “is it set in New York?”, KNN does its job perfectly and Friends ends up next to Law & Order. The algorithm is fine; the features are bad. Choosing what to measure is called feature engineering, and it matters more than the algorithm.

nyc = NearestNeighbors(n_neighbors=len(shows)).fit(shows[["in_nyc"]])
# → equally close to Friends: How I Met Your Mother, Seinfeld,

04
What Netflix really does

Same idea, way more dimensions

Learned scores instead of hand-picked ones

Netflix uses large LLM-style models now, not KNN. But they still turn every show into a long list of numbers (an embedding) learned from what millions of people watch, and shows with similar numbers end up close together.
