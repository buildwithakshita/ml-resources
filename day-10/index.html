02
The cheat sheet

Six ideas, that's all

What it is, the dev version, and the trap

Every gesture demo you've scrolled past is built from these six. The model finds the dots; you write the rules.

  • Landmarka numbered dot

    The pose model returns 33 points on your body, the hand model 21 per hand. Each one has an x, y and a visibility score. That is all you get, and all you need.

    Dev: A list of 33 objects with x, y, z, visibility

    Watch out: The numbering is fixed. Landmark 15 is always the left wrist, so you look it up, never search for it.

    What comes back
    landmark[15] # left wrist x = 0.62 # 0 = left edge, 1 = right y = 0.31 # 0 = TOP, 1 = bottom visibility = 0.98

  • Normalised coordinates0 to 1, not pixels

    Positions come back as fractions of the frame, not pixels, so they survive a resize. But they still change when you move closer to the camera — so divide every distance by something on the body, like shoulder width.

    Dev: Measuring in body-widths instead of pixels

    Watch out: y grows downward. "Hand above shoulder" is wrist.y < shoulder.y, which looks backwards the first time.

    Distance that survives you moving
    shoulder_width = dist(L_SHOULDER, R_SHOULDER) how_far = dist(wrist, mouth) / shoulder_width # now 0.6 means the same thing # whether you're near or far

  • Rule-based classifierif, not fit

    No training set, no labels, no model file. You describe each action in geometry and check the rules in order, most specific first.

    Dev: An if/elif chain over measurements

    Watch out: Order matters. A raised waving hand passes near your mouth, so if you check EATING first, every wave becomes a snack.

    Stretching, in full
    both_up = (l_wrist.y < l_shoulder.y and r_wrist.y < r_shoulder.y) straight = l_elbow_angle > 150 if both_up and straight: return "STRETCHING"

  • Smoothinga vote, not a verdict

    Landmarks jitter a few pixels every frame, so a raw rule flickers between two labels 30 times a second. Keep the last 15 answers and show whichever wins.

    Dev: A deque plus Counter.most_common(1)

    Watch out: Smoothing adds lag. Too long a window and the label arrives after you've stopped doing the thing.

    Majority vote
    votes.append(guess) # maxlen=15 label = Counter(votes).most_common(1)[0][0]

  • Off-frame predictionsthe confident lie

    When a joint leaves the picture the model keeps predicting where it thinks it is — and keeps its confidence high. Sitting close to a laptop, both wrists get guessed above the top edge.

    Dev: Null-checking, except the null looks like real data

    Watch out: This is a real bug I shipped. Wrists guessed above the frame look exactly like raised arms, so it told me I was STRETCHING while I sat still.

    The fix: on screen, not just confident
    def seen(i): return (lm[i].visibility > 0.5 and 0.0 <= lm[i].x <= 1.0 and 0.0 <= lm[i].y <= 1.0)

  • Least-squares fitscoring your circle

    To mark a circle you first need the circle you meant to draw. Least squares finds the centre and radius that fit your wobbly stroke best; the score is how far you strayed from it on average.

    Dev: numpy.linalg.lstsq on the circle equation

    Watch out: Pick what you average carefully. Perimeter-based roundness scores a perfect square 0.785 out of 1 — close enough to a circle to fool a naive threshold.

    Average wander → score
    score = 100 (1 − k · mean |ri − r|r)
    radii = np.hypot(*(pts - centre).T) wander = abs(radii - r).mean() / r
Bonus Is this really AI? The landmark models are trained neural networks — Google trained those. What I didn't train is the part that decides what you're doing. That's the honest split: pretrained perception, hand-written logic on top.
03
Try it in 10 lines

The whole trick

Arms up, in one comparison

This is the line that surprises people. There is no model deciding you're stretching — it's one number being smaller than another.

# pip install mediapipe opencv-python
import cv2, mediapipe as mp
pose = mp.solutions.pose.Pose()
P = mp.solutions.pose.PoseLandmark
cam = cv2.VideoCapture(0)

while True:
    ok, frame = cam.read()
    r = pose.process(cv2.cvtColor(frame, cv2.COLOR_BGR2RGB))
    if r.pose_landmarks:
        lm = r.pose_landmarks.landmark
        # y grows downward, so "above" is less-than
        up = lm[P.LEFT_WRIST].y < lm[P.LEFT_SHOULDER].y
        cv2.putText(frame, "STRETCHING" if up else "...", (40, 80),
                    cv2.FONT_HERSHEY_DUPLEX, 2, (255, 255, 255), 3)
    cv2.imshow("day 10", frame)
    if cv2.waitKey(1) & 0xFF == ord("q"): break
04
What went wrong

Both demos lied to me

The useful part of the day

Neither bug was a crash. Both ran perfectly and reported nonsense with total confidence, which is the failure mode you'll meet again and again in ML.

  • The confident ghostSitting close to the laptop, it said STRETCHING while I sat still. My wrists were off the top of the frame and the model was guessing where they'd be — with high confidence. Lesson: a confidence score tells you how sure the model is, not whether the answer is real.
  • The poisoned benchmarkThe slouch detector compared me against the tallest I'd sat in the last 30 seconds. One jittery frame, or leaning back once, set a bar I could never meet — so it called perfect posture slouching for the next half minute. Lesson: never let a single sample define "normal". Use a percentile, not a maximum.

Self-check

Say these out loud

If you can answer all five, you've got it

  1. Why is "hand above shoulder" a less-than, not a greater-than?
  2. Why divide every distance by shoulder width?
  3. Why does the order of the rules change the answer?
  4. A landmark has visibility 0.97 and y = −0.3. What does that mean?
  5. Which part of this was trained, and which part did I write?
