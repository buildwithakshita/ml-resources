Day 12 · The exact plan
I'm following.

I went too fast for the first 11 days and skipped the basics, so I went back and started properly. This is the plan I'm following now, exactly as I use it: 14 steps in 4 stages, 4 projects for your portfolio, and about 10 hours a week. Tick things off as you go.

4 stages4 projects~10 hrs a week

01
How it works

Watch, build, then move on

The whole plan in one sentence

Watch the main course of each stage in order, and finish that stage's project before you move on. Each project gets its own GitHub repo and a README covering what it does, what you learned and your results. By the end, those four repos are your portfolio.

  • Stage 1 · Dec – Jan
  • Stage 2 · Feb – Mar
  • Stage 3 · Apr – May
  • Stage 4 · May onwards
  • PredictionEnd of Stage 1. Your own dataset, classical ML. Later you deploy it with experiment tracking and monitoring.
  • Tiny GPTEnd of Stage 2. A small GPT trained on text you choose.
  • Study buddyBuilt step by step in Stage 3. RAG, then an agent, then deployed with guardrails.
  • RecommenderStage 4. A small ranking or recommendation model, the kind of system behind your feeds.

Stage 1 · Dec – Jan

ML basics

The part I rushed the first time

Math refresher optional: skip it if you're comfortable with the math, come back when you need it
  • 3Blue1Brown · Essence of Linear Algebra
  • 3Blue1Brown · Essence of Calculus
  • 3Blue1Brown · Neural Networks
Main course
  • 1Andrew Ng · Machine Learning SpecializationSkip anything you already know.
  • Project: pick any dataset you like and predict something with it, like song popularity or delivery times.
Papers
  • A Few Useful Things to Know About Machine LearningDomingos, 2012. An easy first paper.
  • XGBoostChen & Guestrin, 2016. Read it while you do the trees section.
Add-ons
  • Stats for ML: A/B testing, p-values, confidence intervals, common distributions.Suggested: StatQuest's statistics videos.
  • Data skills: data leakage, imbalanced classes, feature engineering.Suggested: Kaggle Learn's Intermediate Machine Learning and Feature Engineering. Use them in your Stage 1 project.
03
Stage 2 · Feb – Mar

Neural nets from scratch

Code along, don't just watch

Main course
  • 2Karpathy · Neural Networks: Zero to HeroCode along with every video.
  • 3Karpathy · Deep Dive into LLMs like ChatGPT
  • Project: train a tiny GPT on text you choose.
Papers
  • A Neural Probabilistic Language ModelBengio et al., 2003. Read it with makemore part 2, which builds this model.
  • Batch NormalizationIoffe & Szegedy, 2015. Read it with makemore part 3.
  • Deep Residual Learning (ResNet)He et al., 2015.
  • Attention Is All You NeedVaswani et al., 2017. Read it with "Let's build GPT".
  • Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3)Brown et al., 2020. Read it with the Deep Dive video.
Add-ons
  • PyTorch fluency: write a full training loop (dataset, model, loss, optimizer) without looking anything up.Suggested: the official PyTorch "Learn the Basics" tutorial, or Daniel Bourke's Learn PyTorch for Deep Learning.
  • Computer vision (optional, only if the roles you want need it).Suggested: Stanford CS231n lectures.
04
Stage 3 · Apr – May

Build an LLM app

One project that grows after every playlist

A study buddy that works over your own notes. Each playlist adds one piece to it.

Main course
  • 4Krish Naik · Complete RAGBuild: a chatbot that answers questions from your notes.
  • 5Krish Naik · LlamaIndexBuild: improve that chatbot with LlamaIndex.
  • 6Krish Naik · Finetuning LLMBuild: fine-tune a small model to write flashcards from your notes.
  • 7Krish Naik · Agentic AI with LangGraphBuild: turn it into an agent that quizzes you.
  • 8Krish Naik · MCPBuild: connect it to Claude.
  • 9Krish Naik · AWS BedrockBuild: deploy it.
  • 10Krish Naik · AI Safety and LLM SecurityBuild: add guardrails.
Papers
  • Retrieval-Augmented GenerationLewis et al., 2020. Read it with the RAG playlist.
  • LoRAHu et al., 2021. Read it with the fine-tuning playlist.
  • InstructGPTOuyang et al., 2022. How RLHF turned GPT-3 into a chat assistant.
  • ReActYao et al., 2022. Read it with LangGraph: it's the idea behind most agents.
  • Indirect Prompt InjectionGreshake et al., 2023. Read it with the safety playlist.
Add-on
  • Production ML / MLOps: experiment tracking, serving a model behind an API, drift monitoring.Suggested: the DataTalksClub MLOps Zoomcamp, or Made With ML by Goku Mohandas. Use it to deploy your Stage 1 project with MLflow or Weights & Biases.
05
Stage 4 · May onwards

Job-ready and interview prep

Alongside applying

  • 11Recommender systems and ranking. Do this one first: a lot of ML engineer roles at big tech are recommendations, search or ads.Suggested: Google's Recommendation Systems course, plus Eugene Yan's blog on recsys. Project 4: a recommender on the MovieLens dataset, starting with matrix factorization, then a two-tower model if you have time.
  • 12LLM evals.Suggested: Hamel Husain's Your AI Product Needs Evals. Then add a small test set to your study buddy app.
  • 13ML system design.Suggested: Chip Huyen's book Designing Machine Learning Systems. Practise 3–4 designs out loud, like a recommendation feed, search, or a RAG assistant.
  • 14ML coding rounds: implement k-means, logistic regression and attention from scratch, timed and without notes.Suggested: Deep-ML for ML coding problems, and NeetCode 150 to keep LeetCode fresh.

The rules

What keeps it actually working

The second one is the one I skipped

  • One note per video5 lines in your own words, plus 3 questions to quiz yourself on later.
  • A paper every 2 weeksAbstract and figures first, then the method. Skip the parts that lose you, and write the same 5-line note.
